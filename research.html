Research

How we study water, ecosystems & climate

Our work spans plot-level ecohydrology to continental large-sample analysis, bridging process understanding with data-driven prediction to inform sustainable management in a changing world.

01

Large-sample hydrology

Rather than studying single basins in isolation, we build and analyse datasets spanning hundreds of catchments. This large-sample view reveals which hydrological behaviours generalise, which are regional, and where models succeed or fail. Our CAMELS-IND dataset brings 472 Indian catchments into this global effort.

02

Process-based & data-driven modeling

We develop physics-informed and deep learning models — LSTMs, Transformers, and differentiable hybrid models that embed hydrological structure into neural networks. The goal is prediction that is both accurate and physically interpretable, including in ungauged and data-scarce basins.

03

Climate change & hydroclimatic extremes

We study compound hydro-climatic extremes, regional flood frequency, and how the statistics of floods and droughts shift under climate change — with particular attention to data-scarce mountain environments where hazards are rising.

04

Terrestrial ecohydrology & ecosystems

We investigate vegetation–water–carbon interactions, ecohydrological indicators, ecosystem services, and vegetation carbon sequestration — connecting the water cycle to ecosystem function and the services people depend on, with a focus on the Himalayas.

04

Water Accounting & Nexus Approach

Our research integrates the Water Accounting (WA) framework and Nexus Approach to provide a comprehensive understanding of water resource management across complex river basins. By leveraging diverse datasets, we quantify key hydrological processes at multiple scales, focusing on water inflows, outflows, consumption, withdrawals, and storage variations.
